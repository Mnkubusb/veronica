# Veronica Batch C — Local fast-path replies, Hindi/Hinglish

Date: 2026-09-16. Follows Batch B. Branch `batch-c`, merged to `master` as one unit.

## Global constraints (apply to every task)

- Brain stays on the user's Claude Code subscription login via `claude-agent-sdk`. **No API key**, ever.
- Confirm-gate stays strict: `veronica/brain/policy.py` `classify()` is the only thing that may auto-allow a tool. Never set `allowed_tools`.
- Speech stays local/zero-key (faster-whisper STT, Kokoro TTS).
- Never add `Co-Authored-By` trailers or "Generated with Claude Code" to commits.
- Tests: `uv run pytest -q` (asyncio auto, `live` marker deselected). No test touches the real mic, speakers, network, models or AppleScript.
- Python 3.12, `uv`. Follow existing module patterns (`veronica/brain/intents.py` for local intents; orchestrator local turns like `_music_turn`).
- Voice UX copy: short, friendly, spoken — no markdown.
- Local fast paths must only fire on **unambiguous, whole-utterance** matches (after the existing `normalize()` — wake-word/"please"/filler stripping — and the clause strategy used by `match_intent`); anything else goes to the brain exactly as today.

## C1 — Local fast-path replies

### Goal
Trivial questions get an answer in well under a second with no brain round trip (today's first-sentence latency is ~3 s).

### Design
New `veronica/brain/quick.py`:
- `QuickReply = tuple[str, str]` — `(kind, reply_text)` where `kind ∈ {"time","date","day","math","battery","volume","social"}`. Function `match_quick(text: str, *, now: Callable[[], datetime] = datetime.now, lang: str = "en") -> QuickReply | None`. Pure/synchronous except battery/volume which are resolved by the orchestrator (see below): for those `match_quick` returns `("battery", "")` / `("volume", "")` and the orchestrator fills the text.
- Phrase sets (normalized, exact whole-candidate match unless noted):
  - **time**: `what time is it`, `whats the time`, `time`, `current time`, `tell me the time`, `what is the time`; Hinglish: `samay kya hai`, `kitne baje hain`, `kitne baje hai`, `time kya hai`, `time kya hua hai`. Reply en: `"It's 3:42 pm."`; hi: `"Abhi 3:42 pm hain."` (12-hour, `h:mm am/pm`).
  - **date**: `whats the date`, `what is the date`, `whats todays date`, `what date is it`, `todays date`, `what is todays date`; Hinglish: `aaj kya tareekh hai`, `aaj ki tareekh kya hai`, `date kya hai`, `aaj date kya hai`. Reply en: `"It's Wednesday, September 16th."`; hi: `"Aaj Wednesday, 16 September hai."` (ordinal suffix helper for en: 1st/2nd/3rd/4th…11th/12th/13th/21st/22nd/23rd/31st).
  - **day**: `what day is it`, `what day is it today`, `what day is today`, `which day is it`; Hinglish: `aaj kya din hai`, `aaj kaun sa din hai`, `aaj konsa din hai`. Reply en: `"It's Wednesday."`; hi: `"Aaj Wednesday hai."`
  - **math** (regex over the normalized candidate; `normalize` strips punctuation so `"12.5"` → `"125"` — therefore restrict to integers and percentages): leads `whats|what is|calculate|compute|how much is|tell me` (optional), then an expression made of integers (≤ 12 digits), spaces and the operators `plus|+|minus|-|times|x|multiplied by|*|divided by|over|/|to the power of|squared|square root of|percent of|% of` — see Task grammar below. Hinglish: `kitna hota hai` / `kya hota hai` as trailing lead (`12 aur 8 kitna hota hai`? — NO: only support `<expr> kitna hota hai` and `<expr> kya hota hai` where `<expr>` uses the same operator words plus `guna` (×), `jama` (+), `ghata` (−), `bhaag`/`bhag` (÷)). Evaluate with a tiny recursive-descent parser (precedence: `^` > `* /` > `+ -`; unary minus; `x squared`; `square root of x`; `p percent of x` = x·p/100) — **never `eval`**. Division by zero → `None` (goes to the brain). Results: integers printed without `.0`; otherwise up to 4 significant decimals, trailing zeros trimmed (`"12 divided by 7"` → `1.7143`). Reply en: `"12 times 8 is 96."` (echo the normalized expression as spoken words: `12 times 8`, `12 divided by 7`, `15 percent of 80`, `square root of 144`, `2 to the power of 10`); hi: `"12 guna 8, 96 hota hai."` — Hinglish echo uses the Hinglish operator words. Numbers > 1e15 or expressions with > 6 operators → `None`.
  - **battery**: `battery`, `battery level`, `whats the battery`, `whats my battery`, `how much battery`, `how much battery do i have`, `battery percentage`, `whats the battery level`; Hinglish: `battery kitni hai`, `battery kitna hai`. Orchestrator runs `pmset -g batt` (via `asyncio.to_thread(subprocess.run …, timeout=5)`), parses `(\d+)%` and `charging|discharging|charged`; reply en: `"Battery is at 72 percent and charging."` / `"… and not charging."` / `"… fully charged."`; hi: `"Battery 72 percent hai aur charge ho rahi hai."` / `"… aur charge nahi ho rahi."` / `"… full charge hai."`. Parse failure → `"I couldn't read the battery level."` / `"Battery level nahi mil paaya."`
  - **volume**: `whats the volume`, `volume`, `volume level`, `how loud is it`, `what volume is it`; Hinglish: `volume kitna hai`, `volume kitni hai`. Orchestrator calls `mac_tools.volume_get.handler({})`; reply en: `"Volume is at 40 percent."`; hi: `"Volume 40 percent hai."`; error → `"I couldn't read the volume."` / `"Volume nahi mil paaya."`
  - **social**: sets → reply lists (pick with `random.choice`, seedable via module-level `_rng = random.Random()` for tests):
    - greet: `hello`, `hi`, `hey`, `hi veronica`, `hello veronica`, `good morning`, `good afternoon`, `good evening`, `namaste`, `namaskar`; en replies: `"Hi Manik."`, `"Hello. What can I do for you?"`, `"Hey there."`; for `good morning/afternoon/evening` reply with the same greeting: `"Good morning, Manik."`; hi: `"Namaste Manik."`, `"Haan, boliye."`
    - thanks: `thanks`, `thank you`, `thanks veronica`, `thank you veronica`, `thanks a lot`, `cheers`, `shukriya`, `dhanyavaad`, `dhanyavad`, `thank you so much`; en: `"You're welcome."`, `"Anytime."`, `"Happy to help."`; hi: `"Koi baat nahi."`, `"Hamesha."`
    - bye: `bye`, `goodbye`, `see you`, `see you later`, `good night`, `alvida`, `phir milenge`, `shubh ratri`; en: `"Bye, Manik."`, `"See you."`, `"Good night."` (for good night); hi: `"Alvida."`, `"Phir milenge."`
    - howareyou: `how are you`, `how are you doing`, `hows it going`, `how are you veronica`, `kaise ho`, `kaisi ho`, `kya haal hai`, `kya haal hain`; en: `"I'm doing well, thanks. How can I help?"`; hi: `"Main theek hoon. Aap batao, kya karna hai?"`
    - whoareyou: `who are you`, `whats your name`, `what is your name`, `tum kaun ho`, `aap kaun ho`, `tumhara naam kya hai`; en: `"I'm Veronica, your voice assistant on this Mac."`; hi: `"Main Veronica hoon, is Mac par aapki voice assistant."`
    - whatcanyoudo: `what can you do`, `what do you do`, `help`, `what can i ask you`, `tum kya kar sakti ho`, `kya kar sakti ho`; en: `"I can answer questions, control this Mac, read your calendar and mail, play music, take notes, control your browser, and remember things for you."`; hi: `"Main sawaal jawab, Mac control, calendar aur mail, music, notes, browser aur yaad rakhne mein madad kar sakti hoon."`
- The `hi` reply variant is used when `lang == "hi"` (see C2: the orchestrator passes the language of the current utterance). Hinglish *phrases* match regardless of `lang` (a user in `en` mode saying "shukriya" still gets a reply — in Hinglish, since the phrase itself was Hinglish: `match_quick` returns the hi variant when the matched phrase came from the Hinglish set).
- Orchestrator: in `one_turn`'s local-intent chain, `quick = match_quick(text, lang=self._utterance_lang)` is evaluated **after** every existing local intent (end/hud/mute/memory/screen/music/note/voice/proactive) and before dictation; on a hit → `self.player.reset(); await self._quick_turn(quick, text)`: resolves battery/volume, `_emit("tool", {"summary": "Quick reply", "decision": "auto"})`, `say(reply)`, and logs the turn to memory (`store.add_turn(text, reply)`) like `handle_text` does. Follow-up window opens as usual. Not barge-wrapped (replies are one short sentence).
- **Precedence guard**: "time" must not steal `"set a timer"` / `"what time is my meeting"` — all matches are exact whole-candidate (or, for math, full-match regex), so these longer utterances fall through. Add explicit negative tests for: `set a timer for 5 minutes`, `what time is my next meeting`, `whats the date of the meeting`, `whats 5 plus 5 in binary`, `hello can you open safari` (clause split: first clause `hello` would match — **rule**: `match_quick` uses ONLY the whole normalized utterance, NOT the clause strategy, so "hello can you open safari" goes to the brain).

### Tests
`tests/test_quick.py` (parametrized phrase tables incl. Hinglish, negatives, math grammar incl. errors and formatting, ordinal suffixes, seeded social choice), `tests/test_orchestrator.py` (quick turn speaks, emits tool card, logs to memory, no brain call; battery/volume resolution with mocked subprocess/handler incl. failure copy), `tests/test_intents.py` untouched.

## C2 — Hindi / Hinglish

### Goal
Speak to Veronica in Hindi or Hinglish; she understands, replies in kind (Hinglish in Latin script when you spoke Hinglish, Devanagari when you spoke pure Hindi), and speaks the reply in a natural Hindi voice.

### Design
- **Language mode** setting/pref: `language ∈ {"en","hi","auto"}`, default `"en"`; persisted in `prefs.json` key `"language"`; `Settings.language` is the env-configurable default. Voice intents (in `intents.py`, `match_language_intent(text) -> str | None`): `speak hindi`, `talk in hindi`, `switch to hindi`, `hindi mein bolo`, `hindi me bolo`, `hindi mein baat karo` → `"hi"`; `speak english`, `talk in english`, `switch to english`, `english mein bolo`, `english me bolo`, `angrezi mein bolo` → `"en"`; `understand both`, `both languages`, `hindi and english`, `hindi aur english`, `auto language`, `dono bhasha` → `"auto"`.
- **STT**: `Transcriber(model_name, language: str | None = "en")` — `transcribe(pcm) -> str` unchanged for compatibility, plus `transcribe_detailed(pcm) -> tuple[str, str]` returning `(text, detected_language)` (`info.language` from faster-whisper; when `language` is fixed it echoes that). Model per mode: `en` → `Settings.whisper_model` (`small.en`) with `language="en"`; `hi` → `Settings.whisper_multilingual_model` (default `"small"`) with `language="hi"`; `auto` → multilingual with `language=None` (whisper auto-detect). Partial STT: `tiny.en` in `en` mode, `Settings.partial_stt_multilingual_model` (`"tiny"`) otherwise. Add `Transcriber.set_language(lang: str | None)` (cheap, just the kwarg). The orchestrator holds an `StTFactory` (`veronica/__main__.py` builds `make_stt(model_name, language)`); switching mode that needs a different model loads it in a thread (`asyncio.to_thread`) while Veronica says `"Ek minute, Hindi load kar rahi hoon."` / `"One moment, switching to English."`; then `"Ab Hindi mein baat karte hain."` / `"Okay, English it is."` / `"Theek hai, dono chalega."`. Mode also applies at startup from prefs (models loaded during warmup). Model download on first use is automatic (faster-whisper); `scripts/download_models.py` gains `--hindi` to prefetch `small` and `tiny`.
- **Per-utterance language**: `Orchestrator._utterance_lang` = `"hi"` if the detected language is `hi` OR the transcript contains Devanagari (`[ऀ-ॿ]`) OR (`auto`/`hi` mode and the normalized transcript matches a Hinglish quick/intent phrase), else `"en"`. Set right after transcription in `one_turn` and passed to `match_quick`, `_quick_turn`, and TTS selection.
- **Brain prompt** (`prompts.py`): append one sentence: `"The user may speak Hindi or Hinglish. Reply in the same language they used: if they spoke Hinglish (Hindi with English words, or romanized Hindi), reply in romanized Hinglish using Latin letters; if they spoke pure Hindi, reply in Devanagari; if they spoke English, reply in English. Keep replies just as short."` Always present (harmless in `en` mode).
- **TTS**: `Synthesizer.synth(text, lang: str | None = None)`; `_options(text, lang)` picks: if `lang == "hi"` or text contains Devanagari → `voice = self.hindi_voice` (new attr, default `"hf_alpha"`, pref `"tts_hindi_voice"`) and Kokoro `lang="hi"`; else current `voice` and `"en-us"`. `asynth(text, lang=None)` likewise. Orchestrator passes `lang=self._utterance_lang` for sentences of the current turn (`_say_unlocked` gets a `lang` kwarg; `say(text, lang=None)`; pipelined `handle_text` synth calls pass it; announcements/chimes/confirm prompts stay `None` → auto by script). `voices.py`: add `"alpha": "hf_alpha"`, `"beta": "hf_beta"`, `"omega": "hm_omega"`, `"psi": "hm_psi"` to `VOICES` under a new `HINDI_VOICE_IDS` list (NOT in `VOICE_IDS` cycling order for "change your voice", but resolvable by name and by descriptor `hindi`/`indian` (+ `male`/`female`): `"hindi"` → `hf_alpha`, `"hindi male"` → `hm_omega`). `"use a hindi voice"` sets `tts.hindi_voice` (not `voice`) and confirms in Hindi: `"Theek hai, ab main aise bolungi."`. Voice submenu gets a separator + the four Hindi names; picking one sets `hindi_voice` (checkmark logic tracks both).
- **Sentence splitter** (`sentences.py`): terminators `.!?` and `।` (U+0964); a boundary is followed by whitespace and then an uppercase Latin letter, a Devanagari letter, or a digit. Devanagari text with no terminators still flushes at end.
- **Local intents in Hinglish** (`intents.py` phrase sets; keep tiny): end → `bas`, `bas karo`, `theek hai bas`, `chup`, `chup raho`, `band karo`, `ruko`, `ruk jao`; mute → `mute karo`, `awaaz band karo`; unmute → `unmute karo`, `awaaz chalu karo`; hud_mini → `chhoti ho jao`, `chota karo`; hud_full → `badi ho jao`, `bada karo`; quit → `quit karo`, `band ho jao` (confirm prompt unchanged, English). Confirm words: add `haan`, `ha`, `haanji`, `ji haan`, `theek hai`, `karo` (yes) and `nahi`, `nahin`, `mat karo`, `rehne do` (no) to `Orchestrator.is_confirmation`'s tables. Note `normalize()` strips punctuation and lowercases — all Hinglish phrases are ASCII already.
- **Wake word**: unchanged (`tiny.en` handles "Veronica").
- HUD: shows Devanagari/Hinglish text as-is (fonts are system; verify the HUD CSS `font-family` falls back to a Devanagari-capable font — add `"Noto Sans Devanagari", "Devanagari Sangam MN"` to the stack).
- README: "Hindi & Hinglish" section (how to switch, what to expect, first-time model download ~500 MB for `small` + `tiny`).
